TAB 1
Paste Your Own Sequences

Use this tab when you already have the homeolog sequences (e.g. the 3A, 3B and 3D copies). GSP aligns them, finds positions unique to each sequence's 3′ end, and designs primers anchored at those positions. No genome database needed.

1
Paste two or more homeologous sequences in FASTA format (each must start with >header). You can also upload FASTA files.
2
Adjust the amplicon size range if needed (default 150–1500 bp).
3
Click Design Primers — results appear as tabs, one per input sequence, ranked by specificity score.
4
Click any primer pair row to highlight its position in the alignment viewer below the results table.
5
Click Download Excel to export all primer pairs.
Note: Tab 1 tests specificity only against the sequences you provide. For genome-wide specificity verification, use Tab 2.
TAB 2
BLAST Against Genome

Use this tab when you have a single query sequence and want primers verified genome-wide. GSP BLASTs your sequence against a reference genome, extracts all homeologous hits, aligns them, and identifies positions unique to your query's 3′ end.

1
Paste a single FASTA sequence (one entry only) into the query box.
2
Select a reference genome from the dropdown (e.g. Chinese Spring IWGSC RefSeq v2.1).
3
Click Run BLAST + Design Primers — a live progress bar tracks the job. Large genomes (wheat ~14 GB) take 1–5 minutes.
4
Results are grouped by chromosome. Primers marked 3′-specific have their 3′ end verified genome-wide.
5
Click Download Excel to get a multi-sheet workbook (one sheet per chromosome group).
Tip: If BLAST takes longer than expected, the progress bar will keep updating. Do not refresh — refreshing will cancel the job.
TAB 3
KASP Marker Design

Use this tab to design KASP genotyping assays from two parental sequences. GSP detects SNPs, lets you choose which to target, and outputs FAM and HEX allele-specific primers plus a common primer — ready to order.

1
Paste Parent 1 (Wild Type) in the top box and Parent 2 (Mutant) in the bottom box — plain sequence or FASTA.
2
Click Align & Find SNPs — SNP positions appear as clickable chips (e.g. pos 142: A/T).
3
Click one or more SNP chips to select them, then click Design KASP Markers.
4
Each marker shows a FAM primer, HEX primer, and common primer. Click any row to see primer positions in the alignment viewer.
5
Click Download Excel to export all markers ready for synthesis submission.
Note: SNP positions are shown in 1-based coordinates. Both parent sequences should cover the same genomic region but do not need to be the same length.
AI
GSP Assistant

The flask icon in the bottom-right opens the AI assistant. It can find genes, extract sequences, and launch primer design automatically — all in plain English.

1
Click the flask icon in the bottom-right corner to open the chat panel.
2
Type your request — e.g. "I want primers for TaGW2 in the Jagger genome"
3
Click a gene in the table the chatbot returns — it extracts the genomic sequence automatically.
4
Type "yes" to confirm — primer design launches in Tab 2 automatically.
✓ Can do: find genes by name or coordinates · extract genomic sequences · launch primer design · explain results · answer primer design and plant genomics questions
✗ Won't do: answer questions unrelated to GSP or plant genomics
TAB 1 & 2
Primer pair columns
3′-end L / R
Y = primer's 3′ end is unique genome-wide. Always prefer Y/Y pairs.
Score
Combined rank: Tm balance, GC%, amplicon size, unique positions. Higher = better.
Unique sites
Positions in the MSA unique to this primer vs all homeologs. More = more robust.
Tm L / R
Melting temperature (°C). Aim for both primers within 2°C of each other.
TAB 3
KASP marker columns
FAM primer
Allele-specific primer for allele 1 (WT). 3′ end sits exactly at the SNP.
HEX primer
Allele-specific primer for allele 2 (Mutant). 3′ end sits exactly at the SNP.
Common
Shared reverse (or forward) primer used with both FAM and HEX.
⚠ Common errors
"No specific pairs found" — try a different region; intronic sequences have more unique positions.
"No unique positions" — check you didn't paste duplicates of the same homeolog.
"Sequence too short" — use ≥100 bp; ideally ≥500 bp.
Tab 2 stuck >10 min — refresh and try again.
💡 Tips
Use genomic sequences (with introns) — more unique positions than exons.
For wheat, provide A, B and D copies in Tab 1 for best discrimination.
In Tab 3, SNP positions are 1-based. Sequences can differ in length.
